# Founder-led selling

Every other skill here writes as "Sellman" — a product face. This one is the exception: it writes as a
named person, because trust in a solo, no-sales-calls business routes through a person a stranger can
look up, not a brand a stranger can't verify. It's a genuinely different register from the rest of this
skill set, and mixing the two voices in one piece of content is the most common way this skill gets used
wrong.

## What's worth posting
- A real number, freshly verified — "found ₹X in leaked revenue across N audits this week" — not a
  progress update with no outcome in it.
- A mistake and what it cost, told plainly. Credibility compounds faster from an honest miss than from
  another win.
- The reasoning behind a decision a reader would also have to make (pricing model, wedge choice) —
  useful to a peer, not just promotional to a prospect.

## Rules specific to this register
- First person, always. No "we" standing in for a team of one — that's a credibility problem waiting to
  be discovered, not a stylistic choice.
- Every number still passes claims-and-compliance before it's posted, exactly as if it were going on a
  GEO page — a personal account is not an exemption from the guard.
- Never post on the operator's behalf without the explicit send step BRAINSTORM.md already requires for
  every channel — this skill drafts, the operator reads their own words before they go out under their
  own name, every time, no exceptions for "it's just a tweet."

## Where it fits the funnel
Founder-led posts are typically the *first* touch a stranger has with Sellman, before they've seen a GEO
page or a marketplace listing — write with that in mind: assume zero context, and let the audit or the
product face pick up the reader from here, not this post trying to close them.
