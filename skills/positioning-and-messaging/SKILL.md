# Positioning and messaging

## Positioning statement (internal, fill before any copy)

For **[ICP]** who **[pain in their words]**, **[face name]** is a **[category they already understand]**
that **[outcome]**. Unlike **[the real alternative — often "doing nothing" or "Stripe defaults"]**, it
**[mechanism-based differentiator]**.

The real alternative is usually not a competitor. For recovery it's "Stripe's built-in retries"; for
renegotiation it's "just accept the renewal"; for Tally it's "the accountant catches it at year end".

## Voice-of-customer first
Before writing, collect 10–20 verbatim phrases from: audit free-text answers, community posts, marketplace
reviews of competitors, support questions. Use their nouns. Store them in `insights` with source links.

## Message hierarchy
1. **Headline** — outcome or loss, in buyer words. No number unless proof-backed.
2. **Subhead** — mechanism in one sentence.
3. **Proof strip** — verified metric with sample size and period, or an audit example.
4. **How it works** — 3 steps, first step is always "run the free audit".
5. **Limits** — what it doesn't support.
6. **Pricing in one line** — "You pay X% of what we verifiably recover. Nothing otherwise."
7. **CTA** — one.

## Style rules
- Plain words over clever ones. No "revolutionary", "supercharge", "unlock", "seamless", "AI-powered" as a benefit.
- Specific > general: "expired cards on annual plans" beats "payment issues".
- Say who it's *not* for.
- Write the Indian and global versions separately when currency, gateway, or regulation differ.
- Disclose automation when a message is AI-drafted and sent by a system, where platform rules require it.

## Anti-patterns
- Feature lists without outcomes
- Claims about competitors you can't source with a dated link
- "Trusted by…" without real, permissioned logos
