# Storytelling and case studies

audit-led-selling establishes that the audit itself is the proof mechanism. This skill is what happens
*after* an audit or fix has a verified result — turning that result into something a stranger reads to
the end, instead of a stat they skim past.

## The shape of a case study
1. **The state before**, in the buyer's own words if you have them (from `voice_of_customer` insights) —
   not "they had payment leakage" but the specific complaint or confusion that led them to run the audit.
2. **The number the audit found**, stated once, plainly, with its leak-type breakdown.
3. **What they did with it** — installed, fixed, or (if they didn't) why not; a story that only shows
   wins reads as marketing, not proof.
4. **The verified result**, sourced to a real `revenue` row or fix confirmation — never a projected or
   estimated outcome dressed as an outcome.

## Rules specific to this skill
- Every number in a case study must already be `numbers_used`-declared and guard-passed — this skill
  arranges truth into a narrative, it does not relax what counts as truth.
- Anonymize by default (`Company X, similar size`) unless a named permission exists; never imply
  permission from the fact that data is technically public.
- A story with one sentence of "before" and ten of "how great we are" is not a case study — the ratio
  should favor the buyer's situation over the product's features.

## Where this compounds
The same verified outcome can become a GEO answer-object, a marketplace listing proof point, and a
community reply's one-line evidence — write the full narrative once, then let content-strategist and
listing-manager pull the length and form each surface needs from it.
